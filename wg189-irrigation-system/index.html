Insights

Irrigation is the artificial supply of water to crops, used to enhance farm productivity and reduce a farmer's dependence on monsoon rains. Different terrains and soils require different types of irrigation techniques.

Canal Irrigation (Phad Irrigation)

Source of water: Rivers, reservoirs, lakes, or dams.

Insight: Water is carried through canals to fields. The Phad irrigation system of Maharashtra uses canals and community-managed water sharing to irrigate crops efficiently.

Drip Irrigation

Source of water: Wells, borewells, tanks, reservoirs, or pipelines. Water is usually supplied using pumps.

Insight: Water is delivered drop by drop directly to plant roots through pipes, reducing water wastage and improving crop growth.

Sprinkler Irrigation

Source of water: Wells, borewells, tanks, reservoirs, or pipelines. Water is usually supplied using pumps.

Insight: Water is sprayed over crops through rotating sprinklers, creating an effect like rainfall. It is suitable for uneven and sandy terrain.

Bamboo Drip Irrigation

Source of water: Natural springs and hill streams.

Insight: Practised in Meghalaya, this traditional method uses hollow bamboo channels to carry water by gravity and drip it slowly onto crops.
