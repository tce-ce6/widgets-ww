  • Population density (N) is the size of a population — the number of individuals of a species living in a given area at a given time.
  • Natality (B) is the number of births added to the population during a given period.
  • Immigration (I) is the number of individuals of the same species that enter the habitat from elsewhere during that period.
  • Mortality (D) is the number of deaths in the population during that period.
  • Emigration (E) is the number of individuals that leave the habitat for elsewhere during that period.
  • The population balance equation: N(t+1) = N(t) + [(B + I) − (D + E)].
  • Growth: if (B + I) is greater than (D + E), the population grows.
  • Shrinkage or stability: if (B + I) is less than (D + E), the population shrinks; if they are equal, it stays stable.
